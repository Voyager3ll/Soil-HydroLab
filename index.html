How water, carbon, and nitrogen move through agricultural soils

We combine field observations, process-based models, remote sensing, and machine learning to understand how climate, land use, and management change soil and water quality, and to help reduce the environmental footprint of farming.

PI: Dr. Kang Liang, Ph.D., P.Eng.Fargo, North Dakota

Carbon & greenhouse gases

Improving nitrogen and carbon process representation in SWAT and other process-based models.

Linking crop production, nutrient management, and water across regions.

The SWAT-Carbon model

    Kang Liang, Ph.D., P.Eng.

    Principal Investigator · Assistant Professor of Soil Physics and Hydrology

    Dr. Liang is an Assistant Professor in soil physics and hydrology in the School of Natural Resource Sciences at North Dakota State University. His research combines field observations, biogeochemical models, remote sensing data, and statistical and machine learning approaches to understand how changes in climate, land use, and management practices affect soil and water quality, hydrological dynamics, and ecosystem health.

    He holds a Ph.D. in Hydrology from the University of New Brunswick, where he is also an Adjunct Professor, and is a registered Professional Engineer in New Brunswick.

    Research

    What we study

    Our work links field measurements and models across plot, field, and watershed scales.

    Theme 1

    Water resources & water quality

    Non-point source pollution from agricultural landscapes is a major source of pollution in aquatic ecosystems. Sustainable practices, efficient water use, and protecting water quality are essential for food security, ecological balance, and community well-being.

    • Nitrate leaching under potato rotations
    • Land use effects on nitrate load and water yield
    • Watershed hydrology in the Canadian Prairies
    • Tailwater treatment in constructed wetlands
    Theme 2

    Carbon sequestration & greenhouse gas emissions

    We study how changes in land use and management under a changing climate affect the carbon sequestration potential and greenhouse gas emissions of cropping systems.

    • Soil organic carbon dynamics in the U.S. Corn Belt
    • N₂O emissions from Corn Belt agroecosystems
    Theme 3

    Agricultural management practices

    Best management practices are widely used to reduce the environmental impacts of farming, yet their effectiveness varies. We study the interactions among BMPs, crops, water, nutrients, and climate variability to support choices that are both economically viable and environmentally sound.

    • Conservation terraces and potato yields
    • Crop rotation and nitrogen dynamics
    • Tillage and soil organic carbon
    Theme 4

    Climate & land use change impacts

    Shifting precipitation, rising temperatures, more frequent extremes, and land conversion driven by food demand all change water availability and quality in agricultural regions.

    • Land use conversion in the Canadian Prairies
    • Land use in Atlantic Canada
    Theme 5

    Biogeochemical model development

    We apply physically based models such as the Soil and Water Assessment Tool (SWAT) and the Cold Regions Hydrological Model (CRHM), and work to improve how process-based models represent nitrogen and carbon cycling in terrestrial and aquatic environments.

    • SWAT-C: soil organic carbon
    • SWAT-C: soil inorganic nitrogen
    • SWAT-C: nitrous oxide
